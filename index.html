Common Hazards

Know the Hazards. Be Prepared.

The mountains present real risks, but with the right knowledge and preparation, you can manage them and climb with greater confidence.

Avalanches

Rapidly moving snow can be triggered by natural forces or human activity, burying climbers in seconds.

PREVENTION TIP

Check avalanche forecasts, assess snow conditions, and travel with proper gear.

Altitude Sickness

Reduced oxygen at high elevations can cause mild to severe illness, affecting judgment and physical ability.

PREVENTION TIP

Ascend gradually, stay hydrated, and listen to your body.

Frostbite

Extreme cold can freeze skin and tissue, leading to permanent damage, often without immediate pain.

PREVENTION TIP

Wear proper layers, keep skin covered, and watch for early signs.

Crevasses

Hidden cracks in glaciers can be deep and deadly, even when snow-covered.

PREVENTION TIP

Travel roped, use proper navigation skills, and probe suspect areas.

Rockfall

Falling rock is common on steep terrain, especially in warm conditions or loose, unstable areas.

PREVENTION TIP

Wear a helmet, stay aware, and avoid travelling late in the day.

Whiteout Weather

Low visibility can cause disorientation, making it easy to lose your route or your team.

PREVENTION TIP

Carry a map, compass and GPS, and know when to turn back.

Hypothermia

Prolonged exposure to cold lowers your core temperature, impairing judgment and muscle function.

PREVENTION TIP

Stay dry, wear insulating layers, and seek shelter early.

Dehydration

Cold, dry air and exertion increase fluid loss, even when you don't feel thirsty.

PREVENTION TIP

Drink regularly, even in cold conditions.

Real-World Guidance

Safety Do's and Don'ts

Good habits in the mountains can be life-saving. Here are a few key reminders.

DO

  • Plan and prepare thoroughly
  • Use the right gear and know how to use it
  • Stay aware of changing conditions
  • Keep your group together
  • Make conservative decisions

DON'T

  • Don't ignore weather warnings
  • Don't climb alone
  • Don't push beyond your limits
  • Don't take unnecessary risks
  • Don't hesitate to turn back

Skills in Action

How to Build a Snow Cave

A snow cave can provide shelter in winter conditions. Learn the safety basics before attempting any snow shelter.

1

Choose a Site

Choose a stable area away from avalanche paths and steep slopes.

2

Dig the Entrance

Only attempt with suitable conditions, equipment and experienced guidance.

3

Create the Chamber

Keep the space modest and ensure the snow structure is stable.

4

Add Ventilation

Keep an air vent open and check it regularly.

5

Finish and Insulate

Use a sleeping pad and keep an exit clear. Never use a stove inside.

Key Milestones

Defining Moments

Across centuries, mountaineers have pushed beyond the known, transforming dreams into reality.

1786

Mont Blanc – First Ascent

Jacques Balmat and Dr. Michel-Gabriel Paccard reached the summit of Mont Blanc, the beginning of modern mountaineering.

1865

The Matterhorn

Edward Whymper and his team made the first ascent of the Matterhorn, revealing both the glory and the risk of the mountains.

1953

Everest – Hillary and Tenzing

On May 29, 1953, Sir Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reached the summit of Mount Everest, the highest point on Earth.

1954

K2 – The Savage Mountain

Achille Compagnoni and Lino Lacedelli became the first to summit K2, the world's second-highest mountain.

1978

Everest – Without Oxygen

Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler climbed Everest without supplemental oxygen, redefining human endurance.

How to Self-Arrest

If you fall on a snow or glacier slope, a fast and effective self-arrest can save your life.

  1. 1

    Turn Onto Your Stomach

    Roll quickly onto your stomach, head uphill.

  2. 2

    Grip the Ice Axe

    Hold the axe with both hands, pick facing into the snow.

  3. 3

    Dig In and Apply Pressure

    Drive the pick in and press down with your body weight.

  4. 4

    Spread Your Legs

    Keep legs apart for stability and friction.

  5. 5

    Stop and Assess

    Once stopped, stay calm and assess your situation.
